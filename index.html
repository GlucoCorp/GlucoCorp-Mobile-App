01The problem

Bleeding after birth is the main cause of maternal death in most low-income countries.

45,000
women die from bleeding after childbirth every year. That is nearly one in five of the 260,000 maternal deaths in 2023.1
70%
of the world's maternal deaths happen in sub-Saharan Africa.2
~2 hrs
is roughly how fast a mother can go from stable to fatal once a bleed takes hold.
02Why it is missed

The body hides the bleed until it is almost too late.

  1. The body compensates. It holds blood pressure near normal while blood is lost, so standard vital signs warn late.
  2. The eye misjudges. Blood loss estimated by eye misses about half of cases.
  3. Reserve moves first. Compensatory reserve starts falling with the first blood lost. That is the signal Aya Mama reads.

Our answer

Aya Mama reads the body spending its reserve, and starts the response clock while there is still time.

04Aya Mama

One band and one system, following one mother.

Reserve 88 Steady Normal

Silent

Staff watch the colour, not the number. Amber means prepare, red means go now. Colour always comes with an icon, and when the signal is poor the band says so rather than guess.

Designed, not yet built

The band

Fitted to the upper arm at admission. It reads compensatory reserve from the shape of each pulse, and alarms on its own with no network needed.

Specified, not yet built

The monitoring and referral system

One clinician watches many mothers, ranked by who needs help first. A referred mother's data travels with her. Built to connect to DHIS2.

Built for a moving, labouring woman in a low-resource room

  • Upper-arm placement. A steadier signal under motion than the wrist. The same strap moves to the forearm when needed.
  • Green and infrared optics. Multi-wavelength sensing that works on darker skin. Infrared doubles as a motion reference.
  • Alarms on its own. Detects, decides, displays and alarms on the device itself.
  • Never off the mother. Hot-swappable dual cells, about 24 to 36 hours each.
  • Works with no internet. An on-site LoRaWAN gateway feeds the ward dashboard directly, and syncs to the cloud when online.
  • Fit for the ward. Voice prompts in Kinyarwanda and English. Wipe-clean, fluid-rated, fresh liner for every mother.
05One mother's journey

From admission to discharge, the band never leaves her.

  1. Admission

    The band is fitted to her upper arm on a fresh liner. Bed, language and volume are set, and the signal is confirmed green.

  2. Labour and delivery

    Silencing an alarm never clears the alert, and every response time is logged.

  3. Ward

    It keeps watching for delayed bleeding as observation thins out. Cells swap without taking it off. If she is referred, her data travels with her.

  4. Discharge and reuse

    The liner is discarded. The module is disinfected, charged and fitted to the next mother.

06Where mothers are lost

Six delays stand between a bleed and survival. Five of them only start once it is spotted.

  1. 1

    Diagnosis

    Aya Mama acts here: earlier, objective detection.

  2. 2

    First response

    The full treatment bundle, given at once.

  3. 3

    Escalation

    Calling senior help without hesitation.

  4. 4

    Temporising

    Stopgaps that buy time during transfer.

  5. 5

    Treating the cause

    Atony, tear, placenta or clotting.

  6. 6

    Blood

    Transfusion in time, the hardest step.

The six delays are set out in The Lancet's 2026 series on postpartum haemorrhage.6 Each minute of earlier detection starts the clock earlier for the other five. That is why the referral system is half the product.

07The evidence

Proven in trauma medicine, and tested on our own data.

Trauma medicine

0.90 AUC

The reserve index predicted collapse far better than the cheap perfusion index, which scored 0.56. Reserve tracks oxygen delivery with R² 0.93.

Janak et al., 20157 · Convertino and Koons, 20208

First obstetric evidence

51 women

At caesarean, women who bled had lower reserve. Blood pressure could not tell the two groups apart.

Reppucci et al., 2024, Anesthesia & Analgesia9

GluCorp's own result

0.815 AUC

From pulse shape alone, with heart rate excluded, across 1,570 patients.

Proxy label on public ICU data. Not yet a maternal model. Threshold calibration is underway.

Alongside the calibrated drape, not against it

The calibrated drape

Recommended by WHO and almost free. It measures blood that has already left the body, and it stops at the delivery bed.

Aya Mama

Reads the body's response before blood loss is visible, and keeps watching through the ward until discharge.

In our pilots the drape does a second job. The blood loss it measures becomes the label that trains the maternal reserve model, so every mother monitored makes the next alarm more accurate.

08Progress

Where we are, and what comes next.

Done

  • Built the Aya Mama app MVP and put it on Google Play. More than 50 interviews with mothers and providers followed.
  • Moved to continuous, clinical-grade monitoring because the evidence pointed there.
  • Locked the hardware specification.
  • Validated the waveform pipeline on PhysioNet data. Heart rate came within 0.8 bpm of ECG.

In progress

  • Moving our headquarters to Kigali.
  • Working through the pilot pathway in Rwanda.
  • Working with a hardware build partner in Kenya.
  • Recruiting an obstetrician as independent clinical validator.

What's next

  • A working prototype, from sensor to on-device alarm.
  • Test the upper-arm signal across skin tones, and gateway coverage in pilot wards.
  • Version one of the monitoring and referral platform.
  • Safety, EMC and ingress testing. Ethics and Rwanda FDA investigational approvals.
  • A validation study at a pilot facility, with the drape as reference.
  • A first pilot with more than 100 mothers across 5 to 10 facilities.
